Mercator vs Equal Earth

How big each country looks on the old world map, next to how big it actually is on the new one.

On 4 September 2026 the UN General Assembly adopted the Correct the Map resolution by 164 votes to 1, encouraging the world to move from the Mercator projection to Equal Earth. The resolution is non-binding: it recommends Equal Earth rather than banning Mercator.

Mercator preserves angles, which is why it won out for sea navigation — but it pays for that by stretching area more and more towards the poles. Equal Earth is an equal-area projection, so a country's share of the map equals its real share of the planet's surface. That is why the "new map" column below is simply each country's true area.

How the numbers are worked out

  • True area is the spherical surface area of each country's boundary, computed on a sphere of radius 6,371.0088 km. Equal Earth is equal-area, so this is also the area it draws.
  • Mercator area is the area the same boundary covers in Mercator's plane, calibrated so that scale is true at the equator. Mercator's area scale factor is sec²(latitude), so the number grows without limit towards the poles.
  • Antarctica reaches the pole, where Mercator runs to infinity, so it has no honest inflation figure and is shown unshaded and left out of the ranking.
  • Boundaries come from Natural Earth 1:110m, a coarse dataset chosen for file size — areas land within a few percent of official figures. Natural Earth folds some overseas territories into their parent state, so France, for instance, comes out larger than its metropolitan area.
  • Everything is computed in your browser from a — side-car data file.
